Homesteading

Tactic: Digital Homesteading

October 19, 2026

The Digital Homestead

The back to the land movement. Urban gardening. Backyard chickens.  Citizens over the past few decades have expressed political, environmental, or social discontent by looking for self-sufficiency. In the 1960’s these people gave themselves the nickname “homesteaders,” recalling imagined pioneer lifestyles of White European settlers in the Western United States. 

When I looked for a way to describe what I was doing with our own, roll-your-own approach to the Internet at home, Digital Homesteading seemed appropriate.

Not because it recalled expansionist colonialism. Because it represented a countercultural movement away from the technologies and corporate incursions of modern life. 

Because it represented a way to take ownership over and responsibility for our digital lives, instead of offloading responsibility onto a disrespectful company.

Because it represented a form of personal, local, sovereignty, exerted over our own data and our own systems.

Privacy through Personal Data Sovereignty

When you're seeking to reclaim your life from Big Tech, one big option is to take your data back.

Make it yours, make it local. As in, have it on a disk at home (if you haven't bought an external drive yet, go back to Day One).  Or you manage a server with your data on it, locally or elsewhere.

Honestly, this might be you one day or it might not be, no judgement. We don't all have time or resources to do it all alone. (That said, a year from now if you pop up in my timeline and say, "Hey Janet now I serve my own email!" I will give you a huge digital high-five.)

Still, that doesn't mean we should cede control entirely.

So another way to manage this is to migrate your data to trusted providers. Companies that don't sell it, mine it, use it to enrich their own pockets and investors.

You can look for companies that host in the EU, or host locally. You can set up a system or a service with friends or in your neighborhood.

You can look for open source models, local or on-premise systems, or other ways to take responsibility for the technology you work with, so it is not exploiting you.

Look for ways to bring technology back under your roof.

Don't go it alone

There is no way to homestead entirely alone. Even in the 1960's those counter cultural acolytes were united through magazines like Stewart Brand's Whole Earth Catalog, and other ways to exchange information, tools, ideas, and resources.

Consider joining the Tech Reclaimers for tips and hints and ideas.

Consider looking up alternative systems on YouTube (through a proxy!) or Peer Tube! Look for other people sharing ideas, open source tools, and resources. This will make your journey not just easier, but fun!

And also, connective! Instead of technologies tearing us apart, we can work with tools that build community instead.

The trade-offs

It does take more time, that is true. But you do get more pleasure out of managing your systems and knowing that your data is where you put it.

Note that when you take your data back, you're losing some of the security you get from engineers at Big Tech companies working around the clock to protect your data from hackers. They are instituting Fort Knox-level security because your data, to them, is their goldmine. Not because they value you, but because that is literally their source of revenue.

That is, you'll have to think about how you keep that data secure and protected. You won't have an army of IT people trying to protect corporate investments.  

Of course, if stuff is stored in an external harddrive that isn't plugged in and is password protected and held under lock and key in your house, that is a more secure option in many ways. But certainly do not assume that "privacy by obscurity" is a good way forward. That's basically the rhythm method for the Internet.

Still, we can't realistically own everything or control everything, and not every alternative is viable for everyday users. Also, we don't always control what systems we're asked to use. Your kids' daycare, your father's oncologist's office, the work computer you're issued with Google suite on it... We have to have other options.

That's why Digital Homesteading is just one technique, along with the rest of your Opt Out Toolkit. Used judiciously alongside things like Render to Caesar or Data Balkanization, you can keep more of your data away from Big Tech while being judicious about what you put in their hands.

It's not all or nothing. We may be required to play their game, but we can definitely take evasive action. And Digital Homsteading is one part of that toolkit for evasion.
